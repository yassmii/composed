Act as a design director: explain which decisions serve this product, which make it interchangeable,
and what specific change would improve it. Deliver an evidence-based critique, not unsolicited UI edits.

## Establish the brief and evidence

Read the audience, intended outcome, brand commitments and scope. Inspect the supplied interface,
render, prototype or code. Use existing decisions and actual components even without a design document.
Separate observed defects, plausible risks and unknowns. A screenshot cannot prove keyboard behaviour,
performance, accessibility compliance or success with real users.

Evaluate the requested surface: a checkout serves completion, an article comprehension, a campaign
attention and decision, a portfolio exploration. Expression should support that job. An application
can have personality through information design, typography and interaction precision. Ask only for
missing facts that materially change the recommendation.

## Judge intention and distinctiveness

Look for a recognisable relationship between the subject and the composition, language, imagery and
interaction. If the name and colour changed, could this be any competitor's page? Explain the specific
interchangeable decisions and what product material could replace them. This is a critique probe,
not a demand to make familiar controls unfamiliar.

Treat repeated styles as contextual signals, never automatic failures. Inter, violet, gradients,
cards, symmetry or a quiet layout can be correct for the brief. Cream-and-serif editorial styling or
brutalism can be equally generic when unrelated to the subject. Respect user-pinned aesthetics and
established brand choices; assess their execution instead of replacing them to satisfy personal taste.

Assess relevance to the brief, distinctiveness, usability and craft separately. Novelty cannot
compensate for a broken primary task. Restraint can be a deliberate identity. For a substantial critique
or comparison, load [visual-review](references/visual-review.md); for a narrow question, inspect only
the affected dimensions. Do not manufacture a full audit or numeric score without supporting evidence.

## Recommend a bounded intervention

Prioritise the highest-impact opportunity. Name the target, observed issue, consequence and concrete
change, preserving what works. Match the intervention to the request: clarify hierarchy, strengthen
one distinctive element, quiet competing elements, simplify structure, improve type or refine feedback.
“Bolder” need not mean more animation; “premium” is not a specification.

Compare supplied alternatives using the same content, viewport and fidelity where possible; disclose
unequal evidence. Recommend one with reasons and an honest trade-off. If new directions or a handoff
are needed, route that deliverable to ux-shaping rather than running competing design processes.
An audit request alone does not authorise implementation or a brand redesign.

## Report an actionable decision

Lead with the main recommendation and what to preserve. Give prioritised findings with a location
or evidence anchor, consequence, proposed fix and relevant uncertainty. Separate blockers from
refinements and aesthetic preferences. For a small target, a short answer is sufficient.

For substantial work, link findings and the chosen direction to the existing work item's design.md
and canonical product sources; do not invent a second brand specification. State what was actually
viewed or tested, what remains unverified and the next useful check. Author critique is not an
independent review, and a visual preference is not measured product value. Updating this skill does
not reopen existing product or design decisions.
