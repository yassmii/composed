# Resolve the visual system

Read the dimensions affected by visual creation or refinement. These are decision prompts, not a
mandatory style, stack, component library or full-page redesign.

## Composition and content

Arrange real content by importance and relationships before decorating containers. Use grouping,
alignment, proportion and changes in density to establish rhythm. Choose cards for discrete objects,
lists for comparison, sequences for progression; no universal hero or full-screen section recipe.
Let content length and the task determine section height. Preserve the reading and action order on
narrow screens rather than merely shrinking the desktop layout.

Choose a memorable aspect appropriate to the brief: a relationship made visible, a strong image,
typographic treatment or a precise interaction. Support it with quieter surrounding decisions. A
signature is optional for a local utility control. Do not repeat decoration where it obscures meaning.

## Typography and colour

Define type roles, scale, weights, line length and spacing with real headings, prose and data. Inspect
long labels, numerals, diacritics and fallback fonts. Reuse the brand family when given; familiar fonts
are not defects. Use typography to organise and express, not only to make the headline larger.

Define semantic colour roles and actual token values for the chosen direction: surfaces, text, accent,
borders and states as needed. Test relevant combinations in each supported theme. Do not communicate
state by colour alone. Brand violet, gradients, neutral palettes and bright colours are all available
when justified; no palette proves originality. Extend existing tokens before adding unrelated values.

## Images, material and copy

Use visuals with a clear job: show the product, explain a relationship, establish a place or express
the identity. Choose subject, crop, scale and placement together. Real product evidence is more useful
than decorative stock. Keep source/rights and any illustrative stand-ins explicit; never pass them off
as the client's actual people, product or premises. Generate an image only when useful and supported
by available tools; provide a content plan when the asset is missing.

Write specific headings and predictable actions from the user's vocabulary. Preserve supplied facts;
label proposed copy and demonstration data. Do not fabricate prices, customers or metrics. Error and
empty states should tell users how to proceed. A restrained voice can have personality without jokes
or ornate labels. Captions are useful when they clarify the content; do not ban them by style.

## Interaction and motion

Choose motion to explain state, direct attention or realise a requested expressive experience. State
its purpose and how the static/reduced-motion version retains meaning. Keep feedback responsive,
interruptible where needed and consistent. Do not require entrance animations, WebGL, scroll scenes,
hover lifts or a fixed duration everywhere. Prefer inexpensive animation properties where suitable;
measure performance when the implementation warrants it instead of claiming smoothness from code.

Preserve familiar semantics, keyboard operation, visible focus, meaningful labels and error recovery.
Check the applicable web/native accessibility requirements, contrast, zoom and target spacing; visual
polish does not establish compliance. Make touch and keyboard paths work without hover.

## Render and compare

Inspect representative large and narrow layouts with actual content and the affected states. Check
hierarchy, type loading, image crops, overflow and action reachability. Exercise the changed controls,
focus and reduced motion where relevant. Record source/viewport/state and what was not checked.
Compare to the chosen direction as well as technical checks: passing a linter does not prove the
composition survived. Without rendering tools, leave visual verification explicitly open.
